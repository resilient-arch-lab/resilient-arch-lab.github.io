Welcome to the Resilient Architecture (ResArch) Lab at the University of New Hampshire. Every computing system rests on layers of hardware, firmware, and low-level software, and a weakness at any one of those layers can undermine everything built on top of it. We study how these systems fail, how they can be attacked, and how they can be made to withstand both.

Our work spans the full stack. We investigate side-channel and fault-injection attacks on hardware, find vulnerabilities in firmware and embedded operating systems, and secure safety-critical systems such as connected vehicles. We also build automated repair methods, including approaches that use large language models, to fix defects in hardware descriptions and firmware, and we study how to measure honestly whether those repairs are correct.

The lab is led by Dr. Dean Sullivan, Assistant Professor in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at UNH.

Interested in working with us?

Prospective students and collaborators whose interests overlap with ours are welcome to reach out to Dr. Sullivan by email. A short description of your background and the problems you would like to work on is a good place to start.

Undergraduate Research

Undergraduate students are an important part of the lab. Dr. Sullivan mentors undergraduate researchers through the Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship, the UNH Undergraduate Research Conference, and senior design projects. Mentored projects have included the following.

  • Rowhammer attacks on FPGAs (senior design)
  • Bluetooth frame injection attacks (senior design)
  • Large language models for automated microarchitectural attack discovery (summer fellowship)
  • Remote beehive monitoring with LoRa radio (undergraduate research conference, winner)

Workshop Series

Dr. Sullivan leads the Workshop on Hardware Attack Artifacts, Analysis, and Metrics (WHAAAM), a workshop series that connects academic hardware security research with real-world threats. WHAAAM encourages open, artifact-driven work with a focus on empirical analysis, practical countermeasures, and quantitative metrics, and it favors reproducible research that goes beyond proof-of-concept demonstrations. Topics span offensive hardware techniques, defensive strategies, and measurable security improvements.

The 2025 edition took place on June 22, 2025 at Moscone West in San Francisco, CA, alongside the Design Automation Conference (DAC). The workshop is sponsored by the Design Automation Conference, the National Science Foundation, the University of New Hampshire, and Oregon State University.
